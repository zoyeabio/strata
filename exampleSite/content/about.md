Strata is a theme, and it is also the record of how we build things. We are
a small studio that makes sites for people who publish. Not portals, not
dashboards — sites that carry an argument and stand on it.

The theme is what came out of doing that work for long enough to notice the
same five problems every time. So we wrote them down, once, and pointed
the template at the answers.

## The five problems

Every site we take on comes back to the same place:

- **Images are the bottleneck.** They load slow, they break layouts, and
  they weigh the page down long after everything else has settled.
- **The head is a shopping list.** Fonts, trackers, embeds — each one a
  request, a vendor, and a way for the page to regress without anyone
  noticing.
- **Search results lie.** The title in the index, the image in the share,
  and the picture on the page are three different decisions, made
  independently, by different people.
- **Typography is left to instinct.** Someone picks a size, someone else
  picks a line length, and nobody owns the reading experience.
- **There is no gate.** Nothing fails when a link rots, an image is
  missing, or a page loses its description. It ships, and it is found.

Strata answers each of these in one place and does not answer it again.
The [features page](/features/) has the details.

## On the small

Small sites get neglected, because they are not worth a platform team.
That is exactly backwards. A small site is the clearest place to be
good: few pages, no CMS to hide behind, no committee. If the type is
wrong, you can see it.

Strata assumes you have a handful of pages and you intend to keep them
for years.
{{< /layout/col-left >}}
{{< layout/col-right >}}
## On the long

A site you cannot maintain is a site you will abandon. Strata ships no
build step beyond Hugo, no asset pipeline to babysit, and no dependency
you have to remember to renew. The demo assets regenerate from a single
Python script with no third-party package.

Maintenance is a feature.
