Small sites get neglected because they are not worth a platform team. That
is backwards. A small site is the clearest place to be good: few pages, no
CMS to hide behind, no committee. If the type is wrong, you can see it.

## The trap is the tool

The tool teaches you the shape. Give someone a page builder and they will
make twelve pages with a hero, a card grid, a testimonial, a pricing table
and a contact form, because that is what the blocks ask for. Give someone
an outline and they will make four pages, because four is what the thing
needs.

So we start from the outline. What is the argument? What has to be true
for a reader to believe it? What can be cut without losing it?

## A reading list, not a landing page

> A landing page asks for an action. A reading list offers a subject.
> Most independent sites want the second one.

We have built a lot of sites that failed at being landing pages and then
were fine. The problem was not the design; it was the promise. The page
asked for a signup and the site had nothing to sign up for.

Once the promise is honest the design follows. Four or five pages, each
one about one thing, each one short enough to finish in a single sitting.

## What you can afford

Small means you can afford decisions a large site cannot:

- One typeface, with the weights you actually use.
- A narrow reading column, because nobody reads a wide one.
- No navigation deeper than one level.
- Images that are chosen, not filled.

Each of those is a decision you have to keep making forever. A small site
can afford to keep them because there are so few places to keep them.

## The measure

The measure is not "does it look good". It is whether the fifth page is
worth building. If not, you have four pages, and that is a complete site.
