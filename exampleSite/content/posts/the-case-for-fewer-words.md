The reader has already read a lot today. The page has no claim on more of
their attention than it earns.

This is not an aesthetic argument. It is an arithmetic one.

## The sentence test

Every sentence on a page should earn the next sentence. That is a harsh
standard, and it is the right one, because the alternative is not that
the reader finishes the page. The alternative is that they stop partway
through, and they never come back, and they do not know why.

A sentence earns the next one in a few ways:

- It changes something. A fact, a direction, a question.
- It makes something easier. An example after a claim, a number after a
  estimate.
- It says the next thing out loud. A transition is not filler; it is the
  reader being carried.

If a sentence does none of those, it is either decoration or anxiety.
Cut it either way.

## Titles are the whole thing

A title is the only part of a piece that most people will read. It shows
up in the search result, in the share preview, in the navigation, and in
the table of contents. Everything else is behind it.

So write the title first, and write it as if it is the only sentence
that will ship. It should stand alone. It should not say "How to" if the
thing is not a how-to. It should not promise a comparison it does not
make.

A title that survives on its own makes the rest of the page easier,
because the reader already knows what the argument is.

## The paragraph is a unit

A paragraph is not a visual block. It is one move. One move per
paragraph, and each paragraph has a first sentence that says what the
move is.

This is a writing rule before it is a design rule, but it changes the
design. When each paragraph has a clear first sentence, the page reads
down the left edge, and the left edge is what carries the reader. That
is why a narrow column works: it makes the left edge the whole thing.

## Cut the apology

> Independent sites over-explain, because they are afraid of being
> taken out of context.

Do not explain why something is not there. Do not say "we did not add a
pricing table because our work is bespoke". If it is not there, it is
not there.

Every apology on a page is a place where the site is defending itself
instead of making its case. A site that needs to defend itself has not
finished making its case.

## The number

We aim for fewer than 600 words on a page that is not a document. Not as
a rule, as a smell test: at 900 words you have usually started repeating
yourself, and at 1400 you have definitely started.

The documents get longer. They should. They are different animals, and
they have their own pages.
