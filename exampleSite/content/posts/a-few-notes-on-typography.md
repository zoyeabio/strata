The reading column is the whole site. Everything else is furniture around
it.

This is the least generous thing a designer can say about their own work,
and it is also the most true. Someone will scroll past your hero, past the
nav, past the footer. They will read a paragraph. That paragraph is the
product.

## One decision, then stop

> The first decision is the typeface. The rest are consequences of that
> one, and pretending otherwise is what fills a spec with forty variables.

One typeface. Two weights, or three if you are going to use a light for
display. A size ramp you commit to. If you need a monospace for code,
that is a second family and it is for code only.

Everything after that should follow from the first choice, not argue with
it.

## The measure

Line length is the setting people get wrong most often. Too long and the
eye drops to the next line before the first one ends. Too short and every
line is a word.

The safe range is somewhere around 45 to 75 characters. Aim for the
middle and check it on a phone, because a phone is where "75 characters"
becomes two.

## Hierarchy is a rhythm, not a ladder

Headings are not sizes in a row. They are the beats of a piece. If every
section has an `h2`, then some of those sections are not sections; they
are paragraphs wearing a heading.

A useful test: read the headings alone, in order. Do they make a piece of
writing? If not, you have decoration where there should be structure.

## Dark mode is a palette, not an inversion

Flipping a light theme to dark produces text that is too bright and
backgrounds that are too dark. The right answer is a second palette,
tuned so that the reading experience is the same and the light is not.

Body text on a dark ground should never be pure white. It should be a
little below it, and the links should be a little below that again, so
that the difference is readable without shouting.

## The thing you will not see

You will never see your line-height until someone tells you it is wrong.
Set it and move on. 1.6 for body copy, tighter for headings, and do not
re-litigate it for the ninth time.
